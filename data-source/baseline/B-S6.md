# Meeting Plan: Supervisor Check-in (Thursday, 30 min)

## Goal

Leave with (1) a decision on the dataset risk and a Plan B, (2) a narrowed scope, (3) a verdict on changing the method, and (4) dates for the next steps.

## Before the meeting

- Send a short status email to the company contact, so the supervisor sees you've tried. Ask the supervisor whether they can make a direct introduction.
- Prepare a one-page sheet with: literature status, dataset status (last contact date, no reply for 2 weeks), the three RQs, and two options for the method.
- Check your university's mid-term presentation date beforehand, so you know whether to ask or confirm.

## Agenda

|Time|Topic|Outcome|
|---|---|---|
|0–2 min|Opening: state the goal of the meeting|Shared agenda|
|2–8 min|Status: literature nearly done, dataset blocked|Supervisor knows the risk|
|8–16 min|Dataset and Plan B|Decision on a fallback|
|16–22 min|Scope: 3 RQs or 1?|Narrowed RQs|
|22–27 min|Method: survey instead of interviews?|Yes/no with reasons|
|27–30 min|Next steps, dates, expert, mid-term|Written action list|

## Questions to ask

**Must**

1. "The company dataset is still outstanding. If it doesn't arrive by [date], what's our Plan B? Could I use public delivery data, a smaller sample, or a different case?"
2. "Given the data risk, which of the three research questions should I keep?"
3. "Can I change the method to a survey? Here's why: [see below]. Would this still answer the research question?"

**Should**  
4. "When will the expert be assigned, and who is it likely to be?"  
5. "When is the mid-term presentation, and what should it show at this stage?"

**If time**  
6. "Can we set a checkpoint in two weeks to review progress against a concrete plan?"

## Making the method argument

Your reason is the data problem, not that you're behind. Frame it like this: "Without the dataset, the ML part can't be validated, so the interviews carry the whole study. A survey could reach more logistics professionals in less time and give a broader base for the delay causes. But I want to check whether that weakens the link to prediction." Be ready for the supervisor to say no. In that case, ask what the interviews should focus on.

## Handling the "I'm too slow" feeling

Don't open with it. Bring a realistic timeline instead: what's done, what's blocked, and what you'll do in the next two weeks. Supervisors respond better to a clear plan than to worry. If the feeling is still strong, you can mention it briefly at the end and ask whether your pace is normal for this stage.

## Before you leave

Confirm, in writing if possible:

- The decision on the dataset and Plan B, with a deadline
- The RQs you'll keep
- The method decision
- The expert's name and assignment date
- The mid-term presentation date
- Your next meeting date

Send a summary email within 24 hours to confirm what was agreed.
