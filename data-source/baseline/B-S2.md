# Meeting Plan (30 minutes)

**1. Open honestly (2 min)**  
Say early that you don't have a topic yet, but you've thought about direction. Something like: _"I haven't settled on a topic yet, but I've been thinking about data and web development, and I'd like to use this meeting to narrow it down."_ This shows initiative and heads off the worry that you're unprepared.

**2. Clarify the process (5 min)**  
Get answers to your process questions first:

- Do I need to find an industry partner, or can the thesis be purely academic?
- Where is the topic list, and can you send it to me?
- What should I bring to Monday's kickoff? A short list of ideas, a one-page proposal, or something else?
- What are the key deadlines between now and the proposal?

**3. Share your background (5 min)**  
Briefly describe your dashboard project at the logistics company. It shows practical experience with data and web tools. Ask whether that experience can shape the thesis. If you want to use anything from the job, check with your employer first, because internal data may be confidential.

**4. Talk through topic options (10 min)**  
Bring two or three rough ideas, each framed as a question. For example:

- _Can an interactive web dashboard make a public dataset easier for non-experts to explore, and how would you measure that?_
- _How accurate are simple forecasting models for delivery or transport times using open data?_
- _Which visualization approaches work best for logistics performance monitoring?_

Then ask: _"Which of these fits what you're looking for, and is any of them too broad or too narrow?"_ Ask whether taking a topic from the list is fine. Choosing one you can make your own is usually a good option, especially if it matches your skills.

**5. Agree on next steps (5 min)**  
Leave with clear action items:

- What to prepare for Monday
- A deadline for choosing your topic
- When to meet again

**6. Recap (3 min)**  
Summarize what you agreed on and confirm the next steps out loud. This avoids misunderstandings and shows you're organized.

**Before the meeting:** Write down your two or three ideas, your dashboard experience in two sentences, and your process questions. Bring a notebook so you can capture the supervisor's feedback.
