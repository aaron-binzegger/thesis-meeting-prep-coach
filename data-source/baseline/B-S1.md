## Output (verbatim)

Here's a plan for the 30 minutes, built around the questions in your notes.

**Goal:** Leave with a topic direction, a clear picture of what the pitch needs, and answers on deadlines and meeting frequency.

**Suggested agenda (30 min)**

1. **Opening (2 min):** Say you're there to pick a topic and get clarity on the pitch and timeline.
2. **Pitch both options (10 min):** Give each idea 3 to 4 minutes, then ask which one the supervisor thinks is stronger.
3. **Scope and workload (7 min):** Ask what 12 credits should look like in practice.
4. **Logistics (8 min):** Deadlines, client status, meeting cadence.
5. **Wrap-up (3 min):** Confirm next steps and who does what before the next meeting.

**What goes in a pitch**

A pitch is a short argument for why your project is worth doing and why you can do it. Five slides is a sensible length for a 5-minute pitch:

1. **Problem:** What's broken or slow, and for whom? Example: new hires wait days for answers to basic onboarding questions.
2. **Why it matters:** Who benefits, and how would you know if it worked?
3. **Proposed approach:** Your method in plain terms, such as "fine-tune or prompt an LLM over company documents" or "train a model on sensor data to predict failures."
4. **Data and client:** What data you'd use, whether you can get it, and who the client is (or that it's not confirmed yet).
5. **Evaluation and risks:** How you'd measure success, what could go wrong, and what you need from your supervisor.

**Comparing two LLMs**

This could work as a thesis question, but it's a different kind of project. It usually needs a clear evaluation design and a benchmark, and it may lack the client-facing angle your supervisor expects. Mention it as an option, and ask whether it counts as an applied thesis or whether they'd want it tied to a use case like the HR chatbot. If it's a fallback, you could frame it as "compare two LLMs on the onboarding task." That gives it a real use case.

**Questions to ask your supervisor**

- When is the task description due, and is there a template?
- Which option do you think is more feasible in 12 credits, given that the client isn't confirmed?
- What does a typical workload look like for 12 credits, and what counts as "too much" or "too little" in your department?
- How often do we meet, and what should I have done before each meeting?
- Is Python fine for the implementation, and is it acceptable to use local or open-source models instead of cloud APIs?

**Before the meeting**

- Message your friend to check whether he has time and whether he could get data access. Predictive maintenance depends on that, so it's your biggest risk.
- Write two or three sentences for each idea, so you can explain it clearly in the first minutes.
- Decide which option you lean toward and why. Supervisors respond well to a stated preference backed by reasons.
- Bring the five-slide outline above, even if you only sketch it, so you're not starting from zero when they ask for the pitch.

If the topic is decided quickly, spend the remaining time on the timeline. If it isn't, ask the supervisor to help you choose.
